# HyperInkStudio

HyperInkStudio is a monorepo for a tattoo-industry application built from the experience of running my own tattoo business.

Prior to HyperInk, I ran my own tattoo business, where I built and ran my own website to book flash. I created a similar site for an artist client (another tattoo artists who hired me to create a custom site for them, similar to mine.)

I took these ideas and advanced them steps further, to create a MVP platform for tattoo artists.

## Features

The platform brings together the tools needed to manage the tattooing process, including:

- Booking: Manage tattoo requests for custom or flash requests.
- Flash: Clients can view and purchase flash uploaded by the artist.
- Payments: Support credit card purchases and payment apps such as Venmo and Cash App.
- Client records: Maintain client histories, including sessions, materials used, notes, and other relevant information.
- Artist Page: Artists will have a web hub with a few pages, such as flash, checkout, and an about page.
- Custom Sites: My own tattoo website is built on top of this system. Potentially, this could lead to artists hiring me to build and design their own custom experiences on top of the system, similar to the way I sold a web app based on my website/web app.

## AI/LLM transparency

My customer base will largely be anti-AI, and that is an intentional consideration in how I design and develop this product. I myself have a mixed and nuanced view on the subject.

As applied solely to application engineering and my limited use of the technlogy, I think of LLMs as just another tool. And like any tool it has its strengths and weaknesses.

Here are some AI guidelines and transparency for this project:

### For customers

- AI wont be used for customer service. Period.
- AI wont be used replace any potential humans. Period.
- AI is never used any art I do. Period.
- AI wont be a feature available to HyperInk customers.

### In Development

- AI is used lightly and manually by copying and pasting. No LLM/AI has access to my project, or is used inside of my code editor.

Limited LLM use is used to:

- Aide in tasks like debugging and catching/fixing sytax mistakes by copy and pasting.
- As a brainstorming tool.
- Aide in summarizing docs.
- Aide in writing small, localized amounts of code, reviewed and edited by myself.
  - Side note: I also hand-code frequently. My development workflow has always consisted of a mix of hand-coding and copying/pasting, reviewed and edited for quality and to fit the context.
- Aide in some technical writing. Always edited and reviewed by myself.
- For placeholder text and data.
